# Above-the-fold Patterns — cross-brand library

15 vetted hero patterns. Phase 1 must specify which pattern the hero uses. Phase 2 must propose variants within the chosen pattern family (same structure, different register). Breaking the pattern requires explicit justification.

Each brand filters 5-8 patterns to its idiom via `Content/<brand>/patterns/above-the-fold/`.

---

## 1. Hero with product hero shot (right) + copy (left)

**When to use:** physical products, product launches, ecom PDPs. The product IS the message.
**When NOT to use:** SaaS products without a physical form, content-heavy pages.
**Brand examples:** Apple AirPods Pro, Bose, Allbirds.
**Copy placement:** Headline left, sub below, CTA below sub. Product image right, full bleed.
**Mobile adaptation:** Product above copy, full width. Same vertical hierarchy.

## 2. Hero with copy (left) + interactive demo (right)

**When to use:** SaaS, dev tools, productivity apps. Show the product in action.
**When NOT to use:** content-only pages, brand sites without a working product.
**Brand examples:** Linear, Vercel, Notion.
**Copy placement:** Headline left, value prop, CTA. Live demo right.
**Mobile adaptation:** Demo becomes a static screenshot or short video.

## 3. Hero with full-bleed editorial image + overlaid copy

**When to use:** brand sites, lifestyle products, editorial. Image drives the tone.
**When NOT to use:** when the image distracts from the value prop. When brand lacks strong imagery.
**Brand examples:** Acne Studios, menswear DTCs.
**Copy placement:** Bottom-left or center, over the image. Strong contrast required.
**Mobile adaptation:** Image becomes taller; copy stays over lower-third.

## 4. Hero with persistent navigation as design element

**When to use:** sites with rich navigation, multi-product catalogs, complex information architecture.
**When NOT to use:** simple marketing sites with shallow navigation.
**Brand examples:** Stripe, Notion, Figma.
**Copy placement:** Top-aligned, integrated with nav. Headline below.
**Mobile adaptation:** Nav collapses to menu icon; copy stays prominent.

## 5. Hero with diagram-as-headline

**When to use:** developer tools, infrastructure products, complex systems. The diagram IS the explanation.
**When NOT to use:** products with non-technical audiences. When the diagram is unclear.
**Brand examples:** Cloudflare, Fly.io.
**Copy placement:** Diagram center, headline above, brief sub below.
**Mobile adaptation:** Diagram becomes smaller; copy stays above.

## 6. Hero with typographic-led (no image)

**When to use:** editorial, blogs, content-driven brands. Type IS the visual.
**When NOT to use:** physical products. When the brand lacks typographic identity.
**Brand examples:** Mailbrew, Austin Kleon's blog.
**Copy placement:** Oversized headline, often left-aligned. Sub, then CTA.
**Mobile adaptation:** Same, scaled down.

## 7. Hero with video background

**When to use:** product launches, brand reveals, cinematic brands.
**When NOT to use:** content-heavy pages, slow networks. Always provide poster fallback.
**Brand examples:** Apple product launches, Tesla, Arc browser.
**Copy placement:** Center or bottom-left, overlaid on video.
**Mobile adaptation:** Often skipped on mobile (autoplay disabled, data costs).

## 8. Hero with sticky scroll-driven second beat

**When to use:** storytelling sites, premium products. The hero is the setup; scroll reveals the rest.
**When NOT to use:** short pages. When the second beat isn't earned.
**Brand examples:** Linear changelog, superwhisper.
**Copy placement:** Headline center. Second beat appears on scroll.
**Mobile adaptation:** Same scroll-driven behavior; reduce distance.

## 9. Hero with persistent product (zooms + rotates on scroll)

**When to use:** physical products, 3D-feeling presentations. The product transforms as you scroll.
**When NOT to use:** SaaS without physical form. When performance budget is tight.
**Brand examples:** Tesla Model 3 page, Apple Pro product pages.
**Copy placement:** Product center; copy on either side, changing as scroll progresses.
**Mobile adaptation:** Simplify rotation. Reduce scroll-driven changes.

## 10. Hero with category grid

**When to use:** ecom catalogs, multi-product brands. Show range immediately.
**When NOT to use:** single-product launches. When the grid dilutes the brand message.
**Brand examples:** Allbirds, menswear DTCs.
**Copy placement:** Headline above the grid. Categories are the visual.
**Mobile adaptation:** Grid becomes single column; same hierarchy.

## 11. Hero with testimonial-led

**When to use:** high-trust products, slow-fashion brands, premium services.
**When NOT to use:** early-stage products without customers. When testimonials are fabricated.
**Brand examples:** Patagonia, customer-story brands.
**Copy placement:** Pull quote, attributed. Sub-context, CTA.
**Mobile adaptation:** Same, with quote above the fold.

## 12. Hero with dual-CTA split (above + below fold)

**When to use:** products with multiple entry points, ecom PDPs, SaaS pricing.
**When NOT to use:** simple marketing sites. When the dual CTA dilutes focus.
**Brand examples:** Ecom PDPs, SaaS pricing pages.
**Copy placement:** Primary CTA above fold; secondary CTA below.
**Mobile adaptation:** Single CTA above fold; secondary on scroll.

## 13. Hero with sticky CTA + scroll narrative

**When to use:** long marketing pages, storytelling that requires sustained engagement.
**When NOT to use:** short pages. When the sticky CTA distracts from reading.
**Brand examples:** Arc browser, superwhisper.
**Copy placement:** Headline center, narrative below, sticky CTA visible during scroll.
**Mobile adaptation:** Sticky CTA moves to bottom bar.

## 14. Hero with persistent side rail (table of contents)

**When to use:** docs, long-form storytelling, multi-section landing pages.
**When NOT to use:** short pages, mobile-primary surfaces.
**Brand examples:** Stripe docs, Linear blog.
**Copy placement:** Side rail left; hero copy center.
**Mobile adaptation:** Side rail collapses to inline TOC.

## 15. Hero with rotating feature carousels

**When to use:** products with multiple flagship features. Apple iPhone-style.
**When NOT to use:** single-feature products. When carousel UX is hostile (most cases).
**Brand examples:** Apple iPhone, multi-feature products.
**Copy placement:** Headline + CTA persistent; feature copy rotates.
**Mobile adaptation:** Swipe-driven carousel; persistent headline.

## Per-brand filter

Each active brand picks 5-8 from these 15 for its idiom. Saved at `Content/<brand>/patterns/above-the-fold/<pattern>.md` with brand-specific notes.

Suggested starting filters:
- **DD** — 1, 5, 8, 9, 11, 15
- **RH** — 1, 3, 7, 10, 11
- **HR** — 2, 4, 5, 8, 13
- **TS** — 6, 9, 11, 13, 15

These are starting points, not requirements. Adjust based on what serves each brand.
