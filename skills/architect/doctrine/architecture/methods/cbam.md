Use CBAM only when credible economic evidence could change a choice among architecture strategies.
If costs, benefits, or decision horizon are unavailable and cannot be estimated proportionately,
state that economics are not decision-ready and use scenario/evidence analysis instead.

## Procedure

1. Define decision boundary, time horizon, affected stakeholders, and economic measure. Link each
   strategy to a quality scenario and expected change in response.
2. Estimate incremental cost and benefit from evidence with named sources, assumptions, and ranges
   where uncertainty matters. Include adoption, operation, migration, and ongoing ownership costs
   only when they differ between candidates.
3. Compare strategies on comparable units and horizon. Show how ranking changes under material
   uncertain assumptions; do not manufacture precise utility scores from weak evidence.
4. Recommend a strategy only if the economic result can affect selection. Otherwise record why the
   comparison adds no decision value and continue with the existing evaluation.

## Output

Produce a concise economic comparison tied to strategies and scenarios, with cost/benefit basis,
assumptions, uncertainty, horizon, sensitivity, and resulting decision or explicit lack of
decision. CBAM informs selection; it does not accept risk or override hard constraints.

## Bounded example

For two import-recovery strategies, compare added implementation and operating effort against the
expected reduction in recovery work over the agreed planning horizon. Use observed incident
frequency and recovery effort if available; otherwise show missing inputs and defer numeric
ranking. Do not assign invented savings merely to complete a table.
