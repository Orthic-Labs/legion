# Jurisdiction, Limitation & Filing Fees

Which commission, whether it is in time, and what it costs to file. Tags as in `cp-act-2019.md`: `[VERIFIED 2026-05-14]` vs `[UNVERIFIED]`.

## 1. Which commission — pecuniary jurisdiction

Post the **30 December 2021** notification (Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction) Rules, 2021), the tiers are: `[VERIFIED 2026-05-14]`

| Commission | Value of consideration paid |
|---|---|
| **District** Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission | up to **₹50,00,000** (₹50 lakh) |
| **State** Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission | above ₹50 lakh, up to **₹2,00,00,000** (₹2 crore) |
| **National** Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission | above **₹2 crore** |

**The test is the value of the consideration paid for the goods or services — not the total relief or compensation claimed.** The Supreme Court has upheld this reading. So a complaint where ₹20,000 was paid but ₹5,00,000 is claimed in total still sits with the **District** Commission, because consideration paid (₹20,000) is the yardstick. State the consideration-paid figure plainly in the Proforma and the complaint; you may also note total relief is within the same tier, but the operative number is consideration paid.

## 2. Which commission — territorial jurisdiction (Section 34(2)) `[VERIFIED 2026-05-14]`

A District Commission complaint may be instituted where:
- (a) an opposite party resides, carries on business, has a branch office, or personally works for gain; or
- (b) any opposite party (if more than one) does the above, provided the Commission permits or the others acquiesce; or
- (c) the **cause of action arises, wholly or in part**; or
- (d) the **complainant resides or personally works for gain**.

Limb (d) is decisive for party-in-person filing — the complainant can file in their own home district. Plead the specific limb(s) relied on. Where goods were delivered to, or a service was to be performed at, the complainant's location, (c) also supports the home forum.

## 3. Limitation — Section 69 `[VERIFIED 2026-05-14]`

- **Two years from the date the cause of action arose.**
- Delay beyond two years may be condoned only if the complainant shows sufficient cause and the Commission records its reasons in writing — do not rely on this; file in time.
- A **continuing cause of action** (ongoing non-performance, repeated refusals, an unanswered cure-period notice) keeps the window open and should be pleaded explicitly in the "Cause of Action" paragraph, with the most recent triggering event dated.
- In the Proforma and complaint, state the earliest event, the continuing nature, and the most recent event — and assert the complaint is within the two-year period.

## 4. Filing fees `[VERIFIED 2026-05-14 — slab values UNVERIFIED, confirm against official notification]`

- **Claims up to ₹5,00,000: NIL filing fee.** Confirmed. Typical small consumer claims (e.g. ~₹25,000-₹50,000 total) fall here — free to file.
- Above ₹5 lakh, fees rise by slab (commonly cited: ₹200 for the next band, up to ~₹2,000 around the ₹20–50 lakh band). **The exact slab values are `[UNVERIFIED]` — confirm against the current official fee notification / the e-Jagriti portal before quoting them.**
- BPL families and Antyodaya cardholders: fee exemptions on production of a valid certificate. `[UNVERIFIED]`
- Fee, where payable, is paid through the e-Jagriti portal during filing.

## 6. Common jurisdiction mistakes

- Using **relief claimed** instead of **consideration paid** to pick the tier.
- Pleading territorial jurisdiction generically ("this Commission has jurisdiction") instead of naming the S.34(2) limb and the facts that satisfy it.
- Quoting the pre-2022 ₹1 crore District limit from a stale source.
- Treating an NCH grievance docket as if it stops or affects limitation — it does not; the two-year clock runs regardless.
- Forgetting to plead the continuing-wrong theory when the filing is close to the two-year edge.
