# Internal Communications

Identify communication type, audience, reporting period, purpose, and required format. Use supplied material or relevant sources the user has authorized and the available connected tools can access. Link to authoritative internal sources where possible; never fill missing company facts from guesswork. If access is unavailable, ask for the material needed or mark the gap.

## Progress, Plans, Problems (3P)

Write for leaders and teammates who have some context but need a fast update. Confirm team and dates if missing. Use this format:

```text
[Team] — [dates]
Progress: [shipped work, milestones, outcomes]
Plans: [near-term priorities]
Problems: [blockers, risks, or None reported]
```

Keep each section to one to three concise sentences; use concrete outcomes and supported metrics. Scale detail to audience size: company-wide updates need milestones, team updates may include task-level work.

## Company newsletter

Summarize the reporting period for the whole company. Prioritize leadership announcements, major milestones, company-wide impact, and information employees need; group related items into clear sections. Keep bullets short and link to source announcements or documents. Avoid duplicate items and detail relevant only to a small team. A 20–25 item edition can be a useful target when source volume supports it, not a quota. Adapt formatting for Slack or email.

## Employee FAQ

Select recurring questions that affect a broad group, then answer each in one or two sentences from official communications where possible. Link authoritative sources. Mark uncertainty plainly and flag questions that need an executive or official answer; never present an inference as company policy.

## Other internal communication

For status, leadership, project, or incident updates, put the most important information first, state audience and time scope, use direct language, and include relevant links. Match any supplied company format. For incidents, distinguish confirmed facts, impact, current response, and next update; do not speculate about cause or resolution.
