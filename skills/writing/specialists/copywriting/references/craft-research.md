## Harry Dry Three-Question Gate

Run this on every headline, hero line, subhead, CTA promise, and major claim:

1. Can I visualize it?
2. Can I falsify it?
3. Can nobody else say this?

If a line fails any one question, rewrite it.

Verified source anchors: `[TUMjnmfsPeM:L54]`, `[TUMjnmfsPeM:L217]`, `[TUMjnmfsPeM:L345]`, `[TUMjnmfsPeM:L1467]`.

Operational meaning:

- Visualizable: the reader can picture an object, moment, person, number, action, or before/after.
- Falsifiable: the claim can be checked, disagreed with, or proven wrong.
- Ownable: a competitor could not paste the same line under their logo without lying or becoming vague.

Bad: "Premium tools for ambitious teams."

Better: "Send the client-ready report before the meeting ends."

## Zoom-In Worksheet

Use this when the copy says "premium", "better", "faster", "AI-powered", "trusted", "scalable", "beautiful", "simple", or "seamless".

Rewrite the vague phrase downward until it becomes a concrete thing:

```text
Regain fitness
Get back into running
Run 5K
Couch to 5K
```

The final line should be photographable, demonstrable, or measurable.

## Speed-To-Value Hook Gate

Borrowed from the Storytelling corpus' Callaway/Harry synthesis:

- The topic must be clear immediately.
- The reader must know why they should keep reading.
- Delay, confusion, irrelevance, and disinterest are hard failures.

Use the One-Mississippi test:

- Read the hook aloud.
- If the topic or payoff is still unclear after roughly two seconds, rewrite.
- Delete greetings, throat-clearing, brand-first exposition, and abstract setup.

## Point, Do Not Claim

Prefer pointable evidence over assertion.

Bad: "Trusted by high-performing teams."

Better: "Used by 14 clinic managers to cut missed-call follow-up from next day to under 10 minutes."

If there is no pointable evidence yet, either:

- ask for it,
- mark the proof gap,
- use a weaker claim that is still true,
- or replace the claim with a concrete mechanism.

Never invent proof.

## Every Word Works

Use this as the final line edit:

- Pull one sentence from the paragraph.
- If the paragraph still works, delete or rewrite that sentence.
- Scan every adjective and adverb; if it does not increase clarity, proof, emotion, or action, cut it.
- Remove neutral words. A neutral word is not neutral in conversion copy; it slows the action.

## But/Therefore Sweep

Use this when copy feels flat, especially long-form sales pages, emails, bios, and founder copy.

Search for "and". Ask whether the real relationship is:

- but: tension, obstacle, objection
- therefore: consequence, next step, payoff

Flat:

```text
You upload your files and the system organizes them and you can export the report.
```

Better:

```text
You upload the messy folder. The system finds the invoices, flags the missing totals, and exports the report before your accountant asks twice.
```

## Copy Audit Lens

When auditing, mark each failure as one of:

- unclear action
- vague promise
- unsupported proof
- not visualizable
- not falsifiable
- not ownable
- delayed hook
- competitor-signable copy
- passive CTA
- dead word

Good audit feedback includes the failed lens, the exact phrase, and one concrete replacement.
