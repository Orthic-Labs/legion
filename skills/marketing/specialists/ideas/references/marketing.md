# /marketing ideas-marketing — Tactical + Psychological Ideation

## Two angles in one skill

### Tactical angle
- Channel-mix ideas (SEO + content + ads + community + partnerships + referrals)
- Campaign concepts tied to product/category
- Distribution loops (acquisition / content / referral / retention)
- Lead magnet / free-tool ideas
- Launch sequencing
- Activation hooks

### Source stack

Before inventing, mine at least one real source:

- Customer/sales language: calls, DMs, support tickets, reviews, objections
- Performance data: winners, flops, save/share/comment patterns
- Market surfaces: Reddit, forums, search questions, competitor comments
- Operator proof: screenshots, shipped work, numbers, workflows, customer artifacts

Classify each idea as **searchable** (captures existing demand), **shareable** (creates demand), or **sellable** (moves toward an offer). The best ideas often do two; forcing all three usually muddies the angle.

Score finalists on customer impact, content-market fit, distribution fit, and resource cost. Do not hand back a generic list of tactics with no reason they fit the user's current stage.

### Psychological angle (mental models, cited inline)
- **Cialdini 7 principles** — reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity
- **Loss aversion** (Kahneman) — frame around what they lose, not gain
- **Endowment effect** — let them try/own before they pay
- **Anchoring** — the first number sets the reference frame
- **IKEA effect** — investment in build = perceived value
- **Peak-end rule** — judged by peak + last moment
- **Social proof asymmetry** — testimonials work, but only specific ones
- **Decoy effect** — middle pricing tier shaped to push the high tier
- **Default bias** — change defaults, not behavior
- **Identity congruence** — sell who they want to become, not what the product does
- **Status motivation** — most purchases are status, not utility

## When to use which angle
- "I have no ideas, where to start" → both, tactical first then layer psychology
- "I have a tactic, how do I sharpen it?" → psychology
- "How do I differentiate?" → both, pull psychological frame the competitor isn't using
- "What's the ad/page hook?" → psychology first

## Pairs cleanly with
- `/research-audience` (input — who) → `/marketing ideas-marketing` (what tactic + frame) → `/build-copy` or `/build-ad` (execution) → `/review-ad` or `/review-blogs` (gate)

## Anti-patterns
- Listing 50 generic tactics with no audience grounding (run `/research-audience` first)
- Picking the "fanciest" psychological principle when the obvious one (scarcity, social proof) fits better
- Stacking too many principles in one piece (one principal lens per asset)
